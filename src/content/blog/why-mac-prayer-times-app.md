Most Muslims check prayer times on their phone. That works at home. At work, it doesn't.

You're in a flow state. You're deep in code, or a spreadsheet, or a presentation. Your phone is in your bag or on Do Not Disturb. Dhuhr comes and goes without you noticing.

This is the problem a native Mac prayer times app solves. Not by being another prayer app — but by putting prayer times where you already look: your menu bar.

## The problem with browser bookmarks

Many people rely on IslamicFinder.org or similar sites. You bookmark them. You check when you remember. Three problems:

- **You have to remember to check** — The browser doesn't remind you
- **Requires internet** — No offline calculation, and your location data goes to a server
- **No calendar integration** — Colleagues still book meetings over your prayer time

## The problem with phone apps

Phone apps are great — for home. At work:

- Your phone is often out of sight, on silent, or in your bag
- Notifications compete with Slack, email, and everything else
- Checking your phone during a meeting looks like you're distracted

## Why the menu bar

The macOS menu bar is the most glanceable real estate on your computer. It's always visible, always one look away. Waqtu puts the next prayer countdown there — “Dhuhr in 1h 45m” — quietly updating every second.

You don't open anything. You don't check anything. You just see it, like you see the time.

## What a native Mac app gets you

- **Calendar blocking** — Automatically show as “busy” during prayers in Outlook/Teams
- **Offline calculation** — Prayer times calculated on-device, no internet needed
- **Location privacy** — Your location stays on your Mac, never transmitted
- **Slack status** — Auto-set “Praying” during prayer times
- **Prayer streaks** — Track your consistency automatically

## No account needed

Waqtu doesn't require an account. There's no login, no cloud sync (unless you want it), no data collection. Everything runs locally on your Mac. Your prayer data is yours alone.
