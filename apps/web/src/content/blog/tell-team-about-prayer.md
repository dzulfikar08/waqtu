Starting a new job or joining a new team? Telling people you need time for prayer can feel awkward — especially if you're the only Muslim on the team. Here's how to handle it professionally.

## The key principle: keep it simple

You don't need to explain the theology of salah. Most colleagues just need to know: (1) you'll be unavailable for a few minutes at certain times, and (2) it won't affect your work. Frame it like any other recurring commitment.

## Sample scripts

### During onboarding / with your manager (1-on-1)

> "Quick heads-up: I pray a few times during the workday — usually takes about 10–15 minutes each time. It won't affect my deliverables, and I'll work around meetings. I just wanted to mention it in case you see me step away."

### In a team standup / Slack

> "Hi team — just FYI, I'll have brief windows during the day for prayer (~15 min each). I've blocked them on my calendar so you can see when. Happy to answer any questions!"

### When a meeting conflicts

> "I have a quick commitment at 1:15 PM. Could we shift this 15 minutes later, or would you mind if I joined 5 minutes late?"

## What not to do

- **Don't over-apologize.** Praying is a right, not a favor. You're not asking permission — you're informing them of a scheduling need.
- **Don't be secretive.** Stepping away without explanation creates more questions than a simple heads-up.
- **Don't make it a big deal.** The more casually you treat it, the more casually they'll receive it.

## For managers: how to support your Muslim colleagues

If you're a manager and someone on your team mentions prayer needs:

- Say "thanks for letting me know" and move on — don't make it a thing
- Avoid scheduling recurring meetings during typical Dhuhr times (12–2 PM)
- If you use shared calendars, the busy blocks will handle everything automatically
- During Ramadan, ask if they need any adjustments (many prefer early meetings)
