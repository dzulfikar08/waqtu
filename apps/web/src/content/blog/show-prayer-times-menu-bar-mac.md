The macOS menu bar is the most glanceable spot on your computer — it&rsquo;s always one look away. Putting the next prayer countdown there means you stop missing salah because you forgot to check. Here&rsquo;s how to set it up with [Waqtu](/) in about ten minutes.

## What you&rsquo;ll need

- A Mac running macOS 12 (Monterey) or later — Intel or Apple Silicon
- Waqtu, downloaded free from getwaqtu.com
- About ten minutes

## Step 1 — Download and install Waqtu

Download the Waqtu installer from the [download page](/mac#download). Open the `.dmg`, drag Waqtu into your Applications folder, and launch it. The first time you open it, macOS may ask you to confirm — that&rsquo;s normal for any app installed outside the Mac App Store.

Once it&rsquo;s running, you&rsquo;ll see the Waqtu icon in your menu bar, already showing a countdown to the next prayer.

## Step 2 — Set your location

Click the menu bar icon and open Settings. Allow location access so Waqtu can calculate accurate prayer times, or enter your city manually if you&rsquo;d rather not grant location permission.

Your location is used only for the calculation and **never leaves your Mac**. Waqtu works fully offline.

## Step 3 — Choose your calculation method

Different regions and scholars use different methods to compute Fajr and Isha angles. Pick the one your local mosque uses so the times line up. Waqtu supports the major methods — Muslim World League (MWL), ISNA, Egyptian General Authority, Umm al-Qura, and more. For a deeper explanation, read our guide to [prayer time calculation methods](/blog/prayer-time-calculation-methods).

You can also set the Asr calculation to match your madhab — Hanafi or Shafi&rsquo;i.

## Step 4 — Match your local mosque times

Even with the right method, your mosque may start a few minutes earlier or later. Add a per-prayer minute offset (for example, +5 minutes for Dhuhr) so Waqtu matches your congregation exactly. This is the single biggest reason people abandon prayer apps — and it&rsquo;s a 30-second fix here.

## Step 5 — Enable calendar protection

This is the feature that sets Waqtu apart. Grant calendar access in Settings, and Waqtu will block your calendar during every prayer — on your default calendar, plus Exchange and iCloud. Outlook and Teams then show you as busy automatically, so colleagues stop booking over your salah.

For the full walkthrough, see our guide on [how to block your calendar for prayer times on Mac](/blog/block-calendar-prayer-times).

## Step 6 (optional) — Set your Slack status automatically

If your team uses Slack, add a Slack User OAuth Token in Settings &rarr; Slack. Waqtu will set your status to &ldquo;Praying&rdquo; during prayer times and clear it when the block ends, using Slack&rsquo;s native status expiration. See our [Slack setup guide](/resources/slack-setup) for the exact steps.

## You&rsquo;re set

That&rsquo;s it. You now have a live prayer countdown in your menu bar, your calendar protected during salah, and — if you enabled it — a Slack status that tells your team you&rsquo;re praying. No ads, no tracking, no subscription.

**Will it drain my battery?** No. The menu bar countdown is lightweight and updates quietly in the background.
