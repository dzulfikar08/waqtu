If you work on a Mac, a great prayer times app is the difference between praying on time and quietly missing salah because a meeting ran long. But there are more "prayer times for Mac" options than ever in 2026 — and they are not all built equal.

We compared the five most popular Mac prayer time apps on the things that actually matter to a Muslim professional: whether they live in your menu bar, whether they protect your calendar, whether they respect your privacy, and whether they charge a subscription. Here's how they rank.

## How we ranked them

Every app below was judged on five criteria:

- **Menu bar presence** — Can you see the next prayer at a glance, without opening anything?
- **Calendar protection** — Does it block your calendar and show you as busy in Outlook and Teams?
- **Privacy** — Does it work offline, with no ads and no tracking?
- **Accuracy** — Can it match your local mosque, with multiple calculation methods and per-prayer offsets?
- **Price** — One fair payment, or a forever subscription?

## 1. Waqtu — best overall for Muslim professionals

[Waqtu](/) is the only native Mac app that puts a prayer countdown in your menu bar _and_ blocks your calendar during every salah. For anyone who schedules meetings in Outlook or Teams, that combination is the whole point.

**Best for:** Muslim professionals who use a Mac at work and want their calendar protected automatically.

**Pros:**

- Menu bar countdown to the next prayer, always visible
- Automatic calendar blocks — Exchange, iCloud, and the default calendar
- Teams and Outlook show you as busy with no manual effort
- Slack status sets itself to "Praying" and clears when the block ends
- Fully offline; your location never leaves your Mac
- Multiple calculation methods, plus per-prayer offsets to match your mosque
- One $2.99 intro payment, lifetime access — no subscription

**Cons:**

- macOS only (an iOS companion is planned)

_Verdict:_ If you miss prayers because of meetings, Waqtu is built for exactly that problem. Read more in our guide on [why you need a prayer times app on your Mac](/blog/why-mac-prayer-times-app).

## 2. Sajda — best open-source option

Sajda is a clean, open-source Mac prayer app with a thoughtful interface and a loyal following. It's a strong choice if you value open-source software and don't need calendar integration.

**Best for:** Users who prefer open-source apps and don't need their calendar blocked.

**Pros:** open-source, modern interface, adhan audio, full-screen reminders, free with an optional $3.99 Pro upgrade.

**Cons:** no calendar integration, no Teams/Outlook status, no menu bar countdown, fewer calculation methods.

## 3. Minarah — best for a hard "stop working" nudge

Minarah takes a different approach: instead of a gentle reminder, it can block your screen to force a break at prayer time. That's powerful for focus — but it can be disruptive in a shared or office setting.

**Best for:** Users who want a hard stop, not a gentle reminder.

**Pros:** focus mode forces a screen block, full-screen reminders, free.

**Cons:** no menu bar presence, no calendar integration, macOS 14+ only.

## 4. PrayerCal — best for calendar-only users

PrayerCal is a web tool that generates an ICS calendar feed of prayer times you can subscribe to. It solves the "put prayers in my calendar" problem — but there's no native app, no menu bar, and no reminders.

**Best for:** Users who only want prayer times inside their existing calendar.

**Pros:** works on any platform via ICS, free, calendar-native.

**Cons:** no native Mac app, no menu bar, no reminders, no offline support.

## 5. Guidance — best simple, established viewer

Guidance is one of the longest-running Islamic apps on macOS. It's trusted and simple, with iOS and Apple Watch support, but its interface is dated and it lacks modern features like calendar blocking.

**Best for:** Users who want a basic, cross-platform prayer times viewer.

**Pros:** established and trusted, macOS + iOS + Apple Watch, free.

**Cons:** dated interface, no calendar integration, no menu bar countdown.

## Which Mac prayer times app should you pick?

For most Muslim professionals, the decision comes down to one question: do you need your calendar protected during prayers? If yes, only Waqtu and PrayerCal do it — and only Waqtu does it from a native menu bar app, offline, with reminders. If you just want to see the next prayer, Sajda is a fine open-source pick.
