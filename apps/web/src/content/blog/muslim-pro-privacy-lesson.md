In 2020, it was revealed that Muslim Pro — the world's most popular Islamic app with over 98 million downloads — was secretly selling user location data to data brokers, including a firm that later sold it to the US military.

## What happened

An investigation by Vice found that Muslim Pro was using a third-party SDK to collect precise location data, device identifiers, and usage patterns from its users. This data was then sold through the data broker ecosystem — ultimately reaching government contractors and military intelligence firms.

## Why it matters for prayer apps

Prayer apps need your location to calculate accurate prayer times. This makes them uniquely sensitive — every Muslim's app knows their home location, workplace, and daily rhythm. When a prayer app sells this data, it's not just a privacy violation — it's a safety concern.

## The community's response

Reddit communities like r/islam and r/MuslimLounge were flooded with posts warning users to delete Muslim Pro. The hashtag #DeleteMuslimPro trended. Many users switched to open-source alternatives — but found them lacking features.

- **Calendar stays local** — Calendar events are created directly via macOS EventKit. We never read or transmit your calendar data.
- **Transparent business model** — We make money by selling licenses, not data. One payment. That's it.

## What to look for in a privacy-respecting prayer app

1. **Does it work offline?** — If it requires internet to show prayer times, your location is being sent somewhere.
2. **Is there a privacy policy?** — Read it. Look for mentions of "third-party services," "analytics," or "advertising partners."
3. **Does it require an account?** — If yes, why? Prayer times don't need cloud sync.
4. **Open-source?** — Apps you can inspect yourself eliminate trust issues entirely.
5. **Paid vs free?** — If it's free, you are the product. A one-time payment means the developer is paid by you, not advertisers.
